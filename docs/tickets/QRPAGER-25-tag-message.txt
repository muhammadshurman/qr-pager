task-10a — the staff shell: passkey-first login, an authenticated shell, the
idle lock, and kitchen-screen settings

WHAT THIS DELIVERS

  * S1 passkey-first sign-in, with a password disclosure behind it, a second
    factor, and a restaurant chooser for a person who works at two. One failure
    message for every cause, because distinguishing them answers "does this
    address have an account here".
  * S2 the authenticated shell: the restaurant's name, the person's name, their
    role, a two-entry nav with aria-current, and no chrome at all until the
    restaurant is known.
  * S3 the idle lock for an unattended kitchen screen: the server session is
    destroyed, the data is dropped from the document, and the overlay names
    neither the person nor the restaurant. Recovery needs the credential again.
  * S4 kitchen screens (device settings): issue a credential shown exactly once,
    list what exists, remove with a confirmation that says what happens.
  * `@qrpager/ui` staff primitives: loading, empty, error, offline,
    permission-denied -- none of which can be constructed without a next action.

THE INVARIANT, AND HOW IT WAS PROVEN

  A staff member authenticated in tenant A can reach no route and no datum of
  tenant B. The subject list is DERIVED from the router -- every `/v1` route
  behind `RequireAbility`, 14 of them -- and driven cross-tenant over HTTP
  against another tenant's REAL rows, by all three roles, and by the actor this
  task makes real: a person holding BOTH memberships with A selected, who
  resolves a tenant legitimately and must still get a 404. §11's QA reviewer
  falsified it four ways in a scratch copy before accepting it.

  The 16 `/v1` routes that are NOT behind `RequireAbility` are enumerated with a
  reason and a named covering test each, and the one real cross-tenant lever
  among them -- `POST /v1/auth/tenant`, whose tenant id is in the BODY where no
  path-parameter sweep can reach it -- is driven with beta's real id, twice,
  with a positive control.

  The browser half drives the same sweep from inside a real session with the
  real cookie, and the rule for what counts as a leak is a tested module rather
  than a condition in a spec: `browser/support/isolation.ts`, with seven leak
  shapes driven past it.

AND THE e2e RED THAT WAS NOT A TIMEOUT

  Three red CI runs and two red local ones all said "/staff settled on neither
  the shell nor sign-in within 60s". The helper raced two markers and the app has
  five phases; `loading`, `offline` and `error` render neither. Racing all four
  turned it into a 915ms named failure, and a probe named the cause:
  `/v1/auth/me -> 429`. The suite was measuring the rate limiter. Production
  bounds are unchanged, `RateLimitTest` still drives them at scale 1, and
  `RateLimitScaleTest` proves a hostile `RATELIMIT_SCALE` cannot weaken one.
